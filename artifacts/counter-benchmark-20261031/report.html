53230A USB frequency-rate benchmark

31 October 2026 (Asia/Shanghai). Counter MY59220164, firmware 03.01, option 106; channel 3, continuous frequency mode. Signal readings were approximately 219-237 MHz during testing.

Practical tested setting: 20 us gate (50 kHz nominal). Two one-million-reading streamed runs completed in 20.388 s and 20.190 s (49,049 and 49,529 readings/s). All requested readings were recovered; no invalid numeric readings, buffer-overflow events or instrument errors were reported.

What limits the rate

  • The app-style VISA configuration enables newline termination during binary reads. In this USB session, disabling VI_ATTR_TERMCHAR_EN raised bulk retrieval from approximately 51-60 thousand to 559-656 thousand readings/s, with unchanged 20,480-byte VISA chunks. PyVISA still accounts for the trailing newline; USBTMC provides message boundaries.
  • With that transport adjustment, the 10 us setting delivered one million readings in 14.745 s with the display on (67,819/s), or 14.190 s with it off (70,474/s). It did not sustain 100,000 delivered readings/s.
  • The instrument accepted and read back a 1 us gate in CONT mode, but 200,000 buffered readings took 2.700 s (74,062/s), followed by USB retrieval. Accepting a gate setting does not establish its physical sampling interval or a 1 MS/s frequency stream.
  • Nonstandard 16, 15 and 14 us requests showed inconsistent correspondence between reciprocal gate and measured wall-clock throughput. They are not recommended as calibrated sample rates.

Scope and interpretation

These are instrument-plus-USB diagnostic tests, not full OpenUFC GUI/disk/ADEV benchmarks. Buffered acquisition time runs from INIT to the return of *OPC?; streamed time runs from INIT to receipt of the last sample. These include startup, instrument processing and, for streaming, USB readout. No external gate timing or per-reading timestamps were measured, so these results do not prove uniform physical sampling at the tabulated wall-clock rate. Input readings varied during the tests.

The current OpenUFC application is unchanged and still caps nominal rates at 1 kHz. Higher-rate operation needs the USB binary-transfer adjustment and rate presets integrated and validated in the application. At 50 kHz the one-million-reading segment lasts nominally 20 seconds, followed by a restart gap.

The Keysight datasheet specifies continuous-mode gates in 10 us steps with a 10 us minimum (page 8), and approximately 75,000 frequency readings/s under its stated speed-test conditions (page 13). Firmware accepting a smaller setting is not a validation of performance outside those documented conditions.

Restoration

Every test restored all instrument settings it modified. Final queries confirmed channel 3, CONT mode, a 1 ms gate, 1,000,000 samples, display on, idle state, zero pending readings and an empty error queue. The original learn string is saved as original-settings.scpi. No app settings or source code were changed.
